> Every invoice lists who did the work — by name, title, and rate. When routine, non-legal tasks like filing or scheduling get billed under an attorney's name at the attorney's rate instead of a paralegal's, that's a rate mismatch worth catching. Check the timekeeper's title, the task description, and the rate together, and a neutral, specific question to your firm is usually enough to get a clear answer.

Every invoice from a law firm lists more than a total. It lists who did the work — by name, by title, and by rate. That detail exists so you can evaluate whether the person doing a task was the right person, at the right price, to be doing it. Most business owners never check.

Here's what to check for, and why it matters.

## The pattern

Legal work spans a wide range of skill levels. Complex legal analysis, negotiation strategy, and courtroom argument belong with an attorney. Scheduling, filing, formatting exhibits, basic correspondence, and document assembly are routine tasks that a paralegal or legal assistant can handle — and that firms typically bill at a meaningfully lower rate for exactly that reason.

The issue shows up as clerical work billed at attorney rates: routine, non-legal tasks logged under an attorney's name and billed at the attorney's rate instead of a paralegal's. Sometimes this happens because a firm doesn't have paralegal support and an associate genuinely did the task themselves. Other times, it happens because nobody on the client side is checking whether the rate matches the work.

## How to spot it on your invoice

Look at three things together, not in isolation:

**The timekeeper's title.** Every name on the invoice should have a title next to it. If you only see names and rates with no titles, ask your firm for a rate schedule that maps each timekeeper to their role.

**The task description.** Read what was actually done. Filing, calendaring, proofreading, compiling exhibits, and basic client-status emails are the kind of tasks that don't require a law degree to perform.

**The rate charged.** Compare it against what you know the firm bills for paralegal or assistant time (ask if you don't know — many outside counsel guidelines address rate disclosure by timekeeper, and it's a reasonable ask even without one in place). A routine, non-legal task billed at the top attorney rate on the invoice is the mismatch worth asking about.

## What to do when you find it

You don't need to accuse anyone of anything. A neutral, specific question is usually enough: "Can you confirm whether this task was performed by [name], and whether it was billed at their standard rate for this type of work?" Firms that bill accurately can answer this in one sentence. Firms that can't, or won't, have told you something too.

If this turns out to be a recurring pattern rather than a one-off, it's a reasonable thing to raise directly with your firm before your next matter starts — and a good candidate for a written outside counsel guideline going forward, so the expectation is set in advance rather than disputed after the fact.

## Why this is easy to miss and hard to catch by hand

Rate-to-task mismatches are rarely dramatic on any single line. They're a five-minute discrepancy here, a fifteen-minute one there, spread across a long invoice you're skimming for the total. Catching the pattern means reading every line against every rate — which is exactly the kind of check that's easy to describe and tedious to actually do, invoice after invoice.
