> Travel and administrative work show up on many legal invoices, and neither is automatically improper to bill. What varies is the policy: some firms bill travel at full rate, some at half, some not at all, and administrative tasks like filing or formatting are typically overhead rather than billable attorney time. If you see either on your bill without a stated policy behind it, that's a reasonable thing to ask about.

Not every line on a legal invoice is legal work in the sense most people picture — research, drafting, strategy calls. A meaningful chunk of most bills is travel, administrative tasks, and clerical work that happened alongside the case. None of that is inherently improper to bill. But it's worth knowing how firms typically handle it, because practices vary more than clients usually realize.

## Travel time

When an attorney travels to a deposition, a court appearance, or a client meeting, that time often shows up as a separate line item. Some firms bill travel at the full hourly rate. Others bill it at half rate, on the reasoning that an attorney sitting in a car or on a train isn't doing billable legal work in the traditional sense. Some firms don't bill travel at all.

There's no single standard — this is a policy each firm sets, and it's usually stated in the engagement letter if it's stated anywhere. If your invoice shows travel time and you've never seen the firm's travel billing policy in writing, that's a reasonable thing to ask for.

## Administrative and clerical time

This is the category worth watching more closely. Tasks like formatting a document, organizing a file, updating a calendar, or scanning and uploading exhibits are typically considered overhead — the kind of work a firm absorbs as a cost of doing business, not something billed to the client at an attorney's or paralegal's hourly rate.

When clerical work does show up as a billed line item, the question isn't whether the task happened — it's who did it and at what rate. A paralegal formatting an exhibit binder at $150/hour is a different conversation than an associate doing the same task at $350/hour. This is closely related to the rate-mismatch pattern covered in [paralegal work billed at partner rates](/learning-center/paralegal-work-billed-at-partner-rates/) — the same task, billed at the wrong person's rate.

## What a normal invoice tends to show

- Travel time, if billed, matches a rate and policy stated somewhere in your engagement documents
- Administrative tasks are either absent from the bill entirely or billed at a lower support-staff rate, not an attorney rate
- The line item names the actual task ("prepare exhibit binder for hearing") rather than a vague label that could be legal or clerical work, the same clarity issue behind [block billing](/learning-center/what-is-block-billing/)

## What to ask

If you see travel or clerical-sounding entries and you're not sure how they're supposed to be handled: "Can you confirm your firm's policy on billing travel time, and whether administrative tasks like filing or document formatting are billed separately or included in overhead?"

That single question usually surfaces the answer quickly — either the firm has a stated policy and can point to it, or the entries get adjusted. If the answer raises more questions than it settles, our guide on [how to dispute a legal bill](/learning-center/how-to-dispute-a-legal-bill/) covers how to push further without damaging the relationship.
